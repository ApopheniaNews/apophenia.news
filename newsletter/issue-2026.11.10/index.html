Two bans, one question

People are being locked out of AI. AI is being locked out of places. Both fights turn on the same question.

Hey pattern seeker,

Here is a pattern from this fall. The word is ban, and it points in two directions at once.

One points at you. People keep reporting that their Claude or ChatGPT account is simply gone. A vague safety flag, an appeal that goes nowhere, a second account banned too. They say they were making games, tracing family trees, learning to code.

The other points at AI. Bloomington, Indiana voted to ban artificial superintelligence. In Congress, one tracker counts 16 members committed to a pause or a ban, and the election takes that to 19.

We wrote about each, two weeks apart. Read them side by side and they make the same argument twice.

Limits are fine. A company has to be able to stop real abuse. A city can decide it wants no part of this. The question under both is whether a no can be total and permanent: a lifetime ban with no explanation and no way back, or a federal ban that decides it once for the whole country. Both pieces say it should not.
 

“No lifetime exile by default.”

That line is from the first piece. We think it holds for the technology too.
